School of Traditional Skills · Messaging Analysis

What the Summit Ads Actually Said

Every word that ran on the fall 2023 and 2024 Traditional Skills Summit campaigns: headlines, primary text, link descriptions, on-image overlays and in-video copy. Ranked by what each message cost to get a registration. This is a companion to the full performance report and covers messaging only.

The message that carried both years

Strip away the variants and the account ran one core promise for two years straight: a free online event that teaches practical, doable skills for eating well and living more simply. Every winning variant is a rewording of that sentence. Every expensive variant added something to it.

The single best-performing primary text of either year, by spend and by cost 
Join the online Traditional Skills Summit for FREE, doable strategies for eating well & living more simply.

Grab your free spot HERE!
https://schooloftraditionalskills.com/summit4/

23 words. Carried $26,073 of asset-level spend in 2024, more than every other variant combined, at $1.71 per registration and 2.71% CTR. It does four things and nothing else: names the event, says FREE, promises two concrete outcomes, and asks.

The whole messaging system in five slots.
  • Headline: FREE Online Homesteading Summit! [DATES]! Unchanged in structure across both years.
  • Primary text: the one-liner above, plus rotating variants.
  • Link description: The only virtual conference giving you the homesteading skills you need for an authentic life you love. Join our free event starting [DATE]. Identical wording in both years.
  • CTA button: Sign Up. The only button ever used.
  • Destination: /summit2/ in 2023, /summit4/ in 2024.

Three variants ran in the same year against the same audiences. The 23-word control cost $1.71 per registration. The 84-word "Learn from over 15 trusted experts" bullet list cost $5.21. Benefit angles beat identity and social proof.

The cheapest angle of either year was Benefit / specific skill at $1.30: "Learn the secrets of an efficient kitchen and eat well with more ease." It names a room, a feeling and an outcome. Next came Benefit / practical methods at $1.58. The two angles that absorbed the most 2023 budget, Identity / permission ("You CAN balance homesteading & 'real' life") at $2.49 and Social proof / scale ("BIGGER than last year's, which had 100,000+ people") at $2.30, were the two most expensive angles that got real spend.

The most under-funded sentence in the account.

"Learn the secrets of an efficient kitchen and eat well with more ease" delivered registrations 48% cheaper than the identity variant it ran beside, and received $4,796 against that variant's $27,381. It was the cheapest message of 2023 and got 15% of the budget the most expensive message got.

Where clicks and registrations disagree

Click-through rate is the most misleading number in this whole dataset. The variant with the highest CTR of any English message in either year was one of the more expensive per registration.

The pattern: abstract promise language raises CTR and raises cost. "Empower you towards a healthier lifestyle" got the most clicks of anything written in two years and converted worst of the short variants. "An efficient kitchen" got the fewest clicks of the group and the cheapest registrations. Curiosity is cheap. Specificity is what converts.

Question openers versus statements

Two variants opened with a question ("Ready to eat well and live simply?", "Want to take control of your health and food?"). They averaged $2.86 per registration against $1.69 for statement and imperative openers, at a slightly higher CTR (3.58% against 3.00%). Same pattern: more clicks, worse cost. Only 2 variants and $1,323 of spend, so treat this as a signal to test, not a settled result.

Emoji

Four English variants used emoji ($11,411 of spend, $3.86 per registration) against sixteen without ($92,558, $2.02). That gap is not really about emoji. Three of the four emoji variants are also the long value-stack variants, so length is doing the work. The one short emoji variant, "🌿 Eat Well, Live Simply!", performed fine at $1.52. Emoji are neutral. Length is not.

The opening line pattern that earned the hook.

The two best hook rates in two years, 36.1% and 29.2%, both opened on an incomplete or unresolved statement: IT'S and EVERYTHING IS CHANGING. No product, no brand, no offer, nothing to disagree with. The lowest hook rate, 10.6%, belonged to the six-second still whose only on-screen copy was the event name and dates, and it still carried $75,410 because what it lost on the hook it made back on completion.

The caption lines inside the videos follow one rule: name a thing a person could go do. "how to manage your animals out in a pasture", "real world skills you can use right now", "a free online training where we teach the skills". No adjectives, no aspiration words, no "transform".

The auto-translation problem

2024 ran the whole message in eight languages nobody asked for.

Meta's automatic language translation was active on the 2024 dynamic creative ads and generated Spanish, French, German, Arabic, Italian, Portuguese, Filipino and Vietnamese versions of the headline, primary text and link description. Combined spend was $74.30 across 10 generated primary-text variants plus nine translated headlines and nine translated link descriptions, producing 12 registrations. The German link description cost $21.76 per registration and the French one $10.57. It is trivial money, but it is an English-language event with an English landing page, and the setting should be off.

What changed from 2023 to 2024

2023 messaging
  • Three primary text variants, no URL in the body, "Grab a FREE ticket here" as the ask.
  • Led with identity: "You CAN balance homesteading & 'real' life."
  • Named a person in the copy: "w/Justin Rhodes & more".
  • Leaned on prior-year scale: "BIGGER than last year's, which had 100,000+ people".
  • On-image CTA: Register for FREE.
2024 messaging
  • Sixteen English variants plus auto-translations, URL pasted into the body of nearly every one.
  • Led with the plain offer: "doable strategies for eating well & living more simply."
  • No instructor named in the primary text at all.
  • Scale claim replaced with a value stack: 15 experts, recordings, sponsor discounts, a Harvest Right freeze dryer.
  • Added a second, urgency-led headline and on-image CTA for the closing days.

The two changes worth keeping: the plain-offer control, and splitting acquisition messaging from closing messaging. The two worth reversing: dropping the named instructor from the copy, and letting the value stack expand the primary text past 80 words.

Rules for the next flight

  • Cap primary text at 30 words for acquisition. Every variant over 40 words cost more than $5.00 per registration. Every variant under 30 averaged under $2.10.
  • Lead with a room, a task or an outcome, not an identity. "An efficient kitchen" beat "You CAN balance homesteading & real life" by 48% on cost.
  • Say FREE in the first line. Every winning variant does.
  • Keep the ask literal. "Grab your free spot HERE!" outperformed "To participate, simply register here".
  • Stop reading CTR as a messaging signal. The highest-CTR message of two years was a mid-tier converter. Rank on cost per registration.
  • Write two message sets, not one. Acquisition messaging (plain offer, benefit-led, under 30 words) and closing messaging (urgency, value stack, "it's not too late"). Do not judge them against the same metric.
  • Put a named instructor back in the copy. 2023 said "w/Justin Rhodes & more" and 2024 named nobody. The single cheapest video of 2024 was the one built entirely around a named expert.
  • Turn off Meta automatic language translation.
  • Fix "Its" to "It's" in the closing headline before it runs again.
  • Verify the 100,000+ attendance claim against the registration record before reusing it.
